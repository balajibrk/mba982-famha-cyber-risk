In 2016, a significant cyber-attack occurred on Uber. Hardcoded AWS credentials in a private code repository let attackers reach an S3 datastore; the breach was concealed for a year and hushed with a payout. The attackers obtained hardcoded credentials from a private repository. A private code repository exposed sensitive access keys. The intruders accessed data stored in an aws s3 bucket. The company concealed the breach from regulators and victims. An insider actor abused legitimate access. The breach exposed approximately 57,000,000 records. Uber disclosed the incident only after a prolonged delay, drawing regulatory criticism. The incident response team investigated the intrusion and mitigated the threat. Uber later strengthened its access control controls.
